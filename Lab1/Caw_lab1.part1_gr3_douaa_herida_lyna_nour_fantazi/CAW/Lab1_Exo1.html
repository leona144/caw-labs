Brief History

The Ancient History of Palestine: Palestine's history stretches back thousands of years and is deeply intertwined with some of the world's oldest civilizations. Located at the crossroads of Africa, Asia, and Europe, it has been a vital cultural and trade hub since ancient times. The land was inhabited by various peoples, including the Canaanites, Philistines, and later the Israelites. It is also home to some of the most significant religious sites in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, such as Jerusalem, which holds sacred value for all three faiths.

Under Foreign Rule and the Ottoman Empire: Over centuries, Palestine came under the control of many empires, including the Egyptians, Babylonians, Persians, Greeks, Romans, and Byzantines. In the 7th century, Muslim Arab armies conquered the region, bringing Islam to Palestine. It later became part of the Ottoman Empire in 1517, where it remained for about 400 years. During this period, Palestine was mostly peaceful and diverse, with Muslims, Christians, and Jews living together in various cities, including Jerusalem.

British Mandate and the Ongoing Conflict: After World War I and the fall of the Ottoman Empire, Britain took control of Palestine under a League of Nations mandate. Tensions increased between the Arab population and growing numbers of Jewish immigrants, especially following the Balfour Declaration (1917), a statement of British support for the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people. In 1948, the creation of the State of Israel led to the displacement of hundreds of thousands of Palestinians, an event known as the Nakba ("catastrophe"). Since then, the region has experienced ongoing conflict, with Palestinians seeking recognition, self-determination, and an end to occupation, while efforts toward a peaceful solution continue to this day.
